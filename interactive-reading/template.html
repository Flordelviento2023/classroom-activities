A New Responsibility

Diego had been a shift supervisor for six months, and today his Managing Partner asked him to take on more responsibility for People tasks.

"Starting this week, New Hire Training will be tracked by you," his MP explained. "Every new team member's onboarding must be completed within the first week, and their training progress is reviewed daily through the Learning Tool."

Diego nodded. He'd seen the process before, but never from this side. "What about the Personal Development Plans?" he asked. "Those are reviewed once a month," his MP said. "Each PDP is discussed one-on-one, and any gaps in certification are noted and followed up on."

That afternoon, Diego sat down with a new hire, Priya, who had just finished her first shift. "Your onboarding paperwork was submitted this morning," he told her, "and your first Learning Tool module is assigned for tomorrow. It usually takes about thirty minutes, and it's required before your next shift."

Priya asked what would happen if she didn't finish it in time. "It's not a big deal," Diego said, "but it does get flagged in the system, and staffing pars are affected if too many trainings fall behind. I'll check in with you tomorrow to make sure it's done."

Later, Diego sat in on a hiring interview with his MP. Two candidates had been shortlisted for the Server position, and both were asked the same set of questions. "We always look for people who can be trained quickly," his MP mentioned afterward, "because our staffing pars are tight this quarter, and every new hire needs to be ready for the floor fast."

By the end of the day, Diego realized how much was happening behind the scenes — trainings tracked, certifications monitored, development plans reviewed — all so the floor could run smoothly without the guests ever noticing.
